How to Choose a Water Pump: Flow, Head and Source

Published: 2026-11-15Our Products: Water Pump

Four numbers decide which pump you need

A pump is a simple machine, and choosing one comes down to four numbers: how much water you need per hour, how high you need to lift it, how far the water has to travel, and what the source is. Get those four right and the rest is a matter of matching the engine to the job.

Our range covers petrol and diesel engines, one and two inch ports, centrifugal and self-priming bodies, and a diesel well pump for deep boreholes. This guide works through the choices in the order that actually decides them.

Start with the lift, not the flow

The single most common mistake is choosing a pump for its flow rate and discovering it cannot lift water to the destination. Head is the hard limit: a pump that delivers a large volume but cannot push water over the height of your tank or up to your second-storey irrigation line is the wrong pump.

Our pumps are specified at a maximum total head of 30 m on the 2-inch Centrifugal Pump, 28 m on the 3-inch Centrifugal Pump, 60 m on the Fire Pump, and 50 m on the double-impeller diesel Double-Impeller. The diesel well pump Diesel Well Pump is rated at 30 m.

Practical rule of thumb: add the vertical height of the lift, the height of every bend and elbow, and roughly a little extra for friction in the pipe, then compare that figure with the maximum total head of the pump.

Then size the delivery for the crop

Flow rate decides how long it takes to move a given volume of water, which on a farm is usually a question of irrigation area.

  • The 2-inch Centrifugal Pump delivers 36 m³/h (600 L/min) on a 50 / 50 mm port.
  • The 3-inch Centrifugal Pump moves 60 m³/h (1000 L/min) through a 80 / 80 mm port — the right step up when you are irrigating a larger area or running several lines at once.
  • The Self-Priming Pump handles 50 m³/h (834 L/min) and accepts a 50-80 / 50-80 mm hose, which makes it the flexible choice when the pipe size has to change from job to job.
  • The double-impeller diesel Double-Impeller delivers 70 m³/h (1167 L/min) — the largest volume in the range, on diesel for continuous duty.

Match the pump to the water source

This is where the two body types separate. A centrifugal pump is designed to sit in a source that is already full of water — a shallow well, a sump, a canal, a tank — and it is the simpler, lighter machine. The Self-Priming Pump is the one that can pull water from a source that is not already flooded: it primes itself in ≤ 90 seconds at 7 m at 8 m.

If you do not know whether your source will stay flooded — a seasonal pond, a river level that drops, a well with a fluctuating water table — the self-priming unit removes the question entirely.

For a deep borehole, the Diesel Well Pump is the machine: 8.8 kW / 11.8 HP @ 3600 rpm, a 7 m suction lift, and 5.5 L of tank, at 100 kg.

Then choose the engine by hours, not by habit

All the small pumps run on a petrol four-stroke engine; the heavy-duty units run on diesel. The petrol 2-inch Centrifugal Pump weighs 24 kg and carries 4.5 L, while the diesel double-impeller Double-Impeller weighs 78 kg and adds electric starting for operators who run it through a long day.

Petrol is the answer for intermittent use and light duty. Diesel is the answer for a pump that runs all day, every day, and where petrol storage is a problem.

What is common to every pump we build

The wet end is the same across the range: a cast-iron impeller, an aluminium-alloy pump body, and a mechanical seal made of ceramic and graphite. That is the part that has to survive grit and running dry, and it is why every pump in the range carries a 1 year warranty.
