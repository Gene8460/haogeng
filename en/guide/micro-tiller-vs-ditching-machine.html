Micro Tiller vs Ditching Machine: Which One Does the Job?

Published: 2026-11-15Our Products: Micro Tiller

Two machines that look alike and do different jobs

Walk behind any of these machines and you see the same thing: an engine, a gearbox, two wheels and a set of steel blades or discs. What changes between a micro tiller and a ditching machine is what that frame is built to do, and the answer is not "the one with the bigger engine".

A micro tiller is built to break and prepare soil. A ditching machine is built to move a lot of soil sideways, in one pass, at depth. For an importer or distributor, that difference is the whole decision.

What the tiller is optimised for

The tiller's working tool is a set of rotary blades — 24 dryland blades in 3+1 groups on every machine in the range — that spin against the ground and churn the topsoil. The width it covers is the width of the frame, adjustable: the 170cc petrol 1WG-4.0AQ works 600-900 mm (adjustable), the 178cc diesel 1WG-4.1A works 800-1050 mm (adjustable), and the 186cc diesel 1WG-6.3A reaches 900-1200 mm (adjustable). Depth is adjustable too, up to 180-350 mm on the largest machine.

That combination — a wide, adjustable, relatively shallow cut — is exactly what seedbed preparation, weed control between rows and general cultivation need. The tiller is the machine for the field itself.

What the ditching machine is optimised for

A ditching machine is built around a different geometry. Instead of blades that churn in place, it carries a ditching wheel or disc assembly that cuts into the ground and throws the soil out to the side, forming a channel as it moves forward. The specification is expressed differently:

  • The 170cc Dragon 1WG-4.0ZL cuts 250-400 mm deep and 200-400 mm (adjustable) wide.
  • The 178cc Bull 1WG-4.1ZN reaches 300-500 mm and 200-400 mm adjustable.
  • The 186cc Bull Pro 1WG-6.3ZN goes to 350-600 mm with 200-400 mm adjustable.

These are canal and drainage figures, not seedbed figures. It is the difference between preparing a bed and cutting a trench.

The one thing that surprises people

The ditching machines are not simply a bigger version of a tiller, and they are not slower or weaker versions either. They run on the same air-cooled single-cylinder engine family, the same ductile-iron gearbox with two forward gears and one reverse, and the same friction-plate clutch. The 178cc Bull 1WG-4.1ZN is rated at 4.0 kW / 5.4 HP @ 3600 rpm and weighs 118 kg.

What is different is the frame, the depth setting and the ground-engaging tool. And the machines convert: the ditching frames are built on the same engine class as the tillers, and the specification lists attachment swap times of under 10 minutes. If a customer needs both jobs in a season, one machine with two attachments is often the more sensible order than two machines.

Which one should a distributor stock?

Look at the job, not the market.

  • Soil preparation dominates — vegetable growing, cereals, cotton, maize, seedbed work. Stock the micro tiller range. The 178cc diesel 1WG-4.1A is the natural anchor model for daily tillage.
  • Drainage and irrigation channels dominate — rice, sugarcane, plantation crops, and any land where standing water is the limiting factor. Stock the ditching machine range. The 178cc Bull 1WG-4.1ZN covers most of that demand.
  • Both, seasonally — stock the tiller as the primary machine and offer the ditching frame as an attachment. That keeps the container count down and gives the customer one machine to service.

If the work is canal maintenance only

There is a third option worth knowing: our pumps. A drainage scheme usually needs moving water as well as cutting channels, and the water pump range includes petrol and diesel units for irrigation and dewatering. Many of our customers running ditching machines are running them alongside a pump for exactly this reason.

Every machine described here carries a 1 year warranty. Tell us the crop, the soil and the drainage pattern, and we will tell you which machine we would put on that ground. Start from the home page or the tiller range.

Frequently asked questions

Answers to the questions buyers ask us most.

Is a ditching machine just a bigger version of a tiller?

No. A tiller works the soil with rotary blades, 24 dryland blades in 3+1 groups on every machine in our range. A ditching machine cuts a channel with a ditching wheel or disc that throws the soil to one side as it moves forward. One prepares a seedbed, the other cuts a trench.

Can one machine do both tilling and ditching?

Yes. The tiller frames are built to take a ridger and a ditcher, and on the 170cc Dragon base the frame changes between the two in under 10 minutes. For a customer who needs both jobs in one season, one machine with two attachments is usually the more sensible order than two machines.

Which machine is right for rice paddies and sugarcane?

Drainage and irrigation channels dominate that work, so the ditching machine range. The 178cc Bull 1WG-4.1ZN cuts 300-500 mm deep and 200-400 mm adjustable wide, and the 186cc Bull Pro 1WG-6.3ZN goes deeper still at 350-600 mm.

Do the ditching machines use the same gearbox as the tillers?

Yes. They run on the same air-cooled single-cylinder engine family, the same ductile-iron gearbox with two forward gears and one reverse, and the same friction-plate clutch. The 178cc Bull 1WG-4.1ZN is rated at 4.0 kW / 5.4 HP @ 3600 rpm and weighs 118 kg.

Which model should a distributor stock first?

Look at the crop rather than the market. Soil preparation dominates for vegetable growing, cereals, cotton and maize, so the tiller range, anchored on the 178cc diesel 1WG-4.1A. For rice, sugarcane and plantation crops, stock the ditching range, anchored on the 178cc Bull 1WG-4.1ZN.

Do I need a pump as well as a ditching machine?

Often, yes. A drainage scheme usually needs moving water as well as cut channels, and our water pump range includes petrol and diesel units for irrigation and dewatering. Many customers running ditching machines run them alongside a pump for exactly that reason.

How deep can the ditching machine cut?

The 170cc Dragon 1WG-4.0ZL cuts 250-400 mm deep and 200-400 mm (adjustable) wide, the 178cc Bull 1WG-4.1ZN reaches 300-500 mm and 200-400 mm adjustable, and the 186cc Bull Pro 1WG-6.3ZN reaches 350-600 mm.

What warranty do these machines carry?

Every machine carries a 1 year warranty.
