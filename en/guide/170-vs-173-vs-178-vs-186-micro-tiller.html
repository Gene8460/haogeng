170 vs 173 vs 178 vs 186 cc Micro Tiller: Which Size to Buy

Published: 2026-11-15Our Products: Micro Tiller

Displacement is the number that actually sizes the machine

Buyers often compare machines by the number printed on the box and stop there. That number — 170, 173, 178, 186 — is engine displacement in cubic centimetres, and it is a good proxy for how much work the engine can do before it has to labour. It is not, however, the whole specification. Working width, machine weight and fuel type change what a given displacement is good for.

This guide compares the four sizes in our range so that a distributor can put the right machine on the right order, and a farmer can see why the machine next to the one being demonstrated is either worth the extra money or not.

170 cc — the entry size

The 170cc machines are the lightest in the range and the easiest to move by hand. The petrol 170 Gasoline Micro Tiller 1WG-4.0AQ delivers 4 kW / 5-15 HP @ 3600 rpm, works 600-900 mm (adjustable), and weighs 68 kg. The diesel counterpart 1WG-4.0A gives 2.9 kW / 5-15 HP @ 3600 rpm over 600-1000 mm (adjustable), on 95 kg.

A 170cc machine is the right answer for vegetable plots, garden work, orchard rows and any land where the machine has to be carried rather than driven. It is also the cheapest machine in the range to move in a pickup and the easiest to service.

173 cc — the middle size

The 173cc diesel 1WG-4.05A is rated at 3.5 kW / 5-15 HP @ 3600 rpm with a 800-1050 mm (adjustable) frame, and the Pro version 1WG-4.05A Pro raises that to 3.8 kW / 5-15 HP @ 3600 rpm with a slightly heavier frame at 110 kg. The petrol 173 1WG-4.05B sits at 4.05 kW / 5-15 HP @ 3600 rpm and weighs 78 kg.

Note that 173cc is a middle specification rather than a headline one. If you are building a range, the sizes that open the most doors in most agricultural markets are the 170, the 178 and the 186 — the 173 fills the gap between them.

178 cc — the working size for daily tillage

The 178cc diesel 1WG-4.1A gives 4 kW / 5-15 HP @ 3600 rpm across 800-1050 mm (adjustable) and down to 150-300 mm. It weighs 115 kg, with a 4.5 L tank.

This is the size that most of the daily tillage work in our markets is done on. The frame is wide enough to make good progress across a plot, and the machine is still light enough for two people to load it. For a distributor building a range around one or two models, the 178 diesel is the safest middle anchor.

186 cc — the open-field machine

At the top of the range, the 186cc diesel 1WG-6.3A is rated at 6.3 kW / 5-15 HP @ 3600 rpm, works 900-1200 mm (adjustable), and reaches 180-350 mm. The 186cc Tiger 1WG-6.3B-T adds electric starting to the recoil starter and weighs 140 kg.

The 186 is for open ground, long rows and daily commercial use. It is the heaviest machine here, and it needs a vehicle that can take that weight — but it covers the most ground per pass of any machine in the range.

| Size | Typical duty | Machine to look at | |---|---|---| | 170 cc | Plots, gardens, orchard rows | 1WG-4.0AQ (petrol) · 1WG-4.0A (diesel) | | 173 cc | Mid-size plots, mixed work | 1WG-4.05A · 1WG-4.05B | | 178 cc | Daily tillage on a smallholding | 1WG-4.1A | | 186 cc | Open fields, commercial daily use | 1WG-6.3A · 1WG-6.3B-T |

What stays the same across all four sizes

Every tiller in the range shares the same core construction, and this is the part a buyer should look at before looking at displacement. A ductile-iron gearbox with two forward gears and one reverse, a friction-plate clutch, and 24 dryland rotary blades in 3+1 groups. The gearbox is what decides how long the machine survives daily work, and it is the same design on the smallest and the largest machine we build.

Depth is adjustable on all of them, and every machine in the range carries a 1 year warranty. If you want the full specification for any model, the micro tiller pages list every one, and the home page is the fastest way to reach our enquiry form.

Frequently asked questions

Answers to the questions buyers ask us most.

Which engine size should a distributor put at the front of the range?

The 178cc diesel 1WG-4.1A. It is the size that carries the most daily tillage work, and it is still light enough for two people to load. The 170cc petrol 1WG-4.0AQ covers the small end of the market, and the 186cc diesel 1WG-6.3A covers open-field work.

Is a 173cc tiller better than a 170cc?

Not in a way that matters to most buyers. The 173cc is a middle specification that fills the gap between the 170 and the 178, and it is a machine to consider when you know the market wants that size. It is not the size we would put at the front of a new range.

Does a bigger engine mean a wider blade?

No. Working width is a frame decision, not an engine decision. The 170cc petrol 1WG-4.0AQ works 600-900 mm (adjustable) on a smaller frame than the 178cc diesel 1WG-4.1A at 800-1050 mm (adjustable), and that difference comes from the machine, not the displacement alone.

Do all four sizes use the same gearbox?

Yes. Every tiller in the range uses a ductile-iron gearbox with two forward gears and one reverse, a friction-plate clutch, and 24 dryland rotary blades in 3+1 groups. The gearbox is the part that decides how long the machine lasts.

What is the difference between the 186cc 1WG-6.3A and the 186cc Tiger 1WG-6.3B-T?

The Tiger adds electric starting on top of the recoil starter and weighs 140 kg. It is the same 186cc class of engine, chosen where operators start and stop the machine many times a day.

What is the maximum digging depth across the range?

Depth is adjustable on every model. It runs from 100-250 mm on the 170cc petrol up to 180-350 mm on the 186cc diesel 1WG-6.3A.

Can I order petrol and diesel models in the same shipment?

Yes. The petrol machines are led by the 170cc 1WG-4.0AQ and the 173cc 1WG-4.05B, and the diesel machines run from the 170cc 1WG-4.0A to the 186cc 1WG-6.3B-T. They share wear parts and the same gearbox design, so a mixed container is straightforward to service.

How much more work does the 186cc do than the 178cc?

The 186cc diesel 1WG-6.3A is rated at 6.3 kW / 5-15 HP @ 3600 rpm against 4 kW / 5-15 HP @ 3600 rpm for the 178cc diesel 1WG-4.1A, and it covers more ground per pass at 900-1200 mm (adjustable) against 800-1050 mm (adjustable). It is also the heavier machine, which decides who can load it.
