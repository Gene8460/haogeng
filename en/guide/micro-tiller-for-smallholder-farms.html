Micro Tiller for Smallholder Farms: Sizing for Land Worked by Hand

Published: 2026-11-15Our Products: Micro Tiller

A smallholding is not a small version of a farm

Most buying advice for farm machinery assumes scale: hectares per day, tractor widths, tonnes per hour. A smallholding works on a different logic. The plot is measured in beds rather than fields, the crop is mixed rather than monoculture, the machine has to be moved by hand when the truck is busy, and the season is a series of small jobs rather than one long run.

That changes what "the right machine" means. Here is how to size a micro tiller for land that is worked by one or two people.

Match the machine to the job, not the plot

On a smallholding the work falls into a small number of recurring categories: preparing a seedbed, cultivating between rows, breaking new ground, and cutting a drain at the field edge. A machine that handles the first three well will carry the season.

The measure that matters is working width, because it decides how many passes a bed needs. The 170cc petrol 1WG-4.0AQ covers 600-900 mm (adjustable) — narrow enough to work between vegetable rows. The 178cc diesel 1WG-4.1A covers 800-1050 mm (adjustable), which suits a plot worked from one end to the other. The 186cc diesel 1WG-6.3A reaches 900-1200 mm (adjustable) and is the right choice when the ground is open and the priority is covering area.

Weight matters more than people expect

On a smallholding the machine is carried across the plot, lifted over bunds, and loaded into a pickup by hand. Weight is not a specification detail here; it is a daily cost in effort and in the number of hands it takes.

The lightest machine in the range is the 170cc petrol 1WG-4.0AQ at 68 kg, with a 3.6 L tank. The 170cc diesel 1WG-4.0A is 95 kg — noticeably heavier, and correspondingly more capable in wet ground. The 178cc diesel 1WG-4.1A is 115 kg, which is still a two-person lift into a vehicle for most operators.

If you are buying for a single operator, start with the 170 petrol or the 170 diesel and stop there. If a second person is always available and the work is daily, the 178 diesel is the better long-term choice.

Fuel availability is the one thing to check first

Before anything else, find out what you can actually buy. A machine is only useful if the fuel is available within reach when the field is ready to be worked. Our petrol range is led by the 170cc 1WG-4.0AQ and the 173cc 1WG-4.05B, and our diesel range runs from the 170cc 1WG-4.0A up to the 186cc 1WG-6.3B-T, which also adds electric starting if you prefer not to pull the starter.

The diesel machines are the better answer for continuous daily work, because a compression-ignition engine holds its speed under load. The petrol machines win on weight and on availability of fuel.

Attachments turn one machine into a season

Every tiller in the range is built to take a ridger and a ditcher, and depending on the model a potato digger, a water pump or a trailer. On a smallholding this is the cheapest way to cover the whole season: one engine, one gearbox to service, several jobs done.

The frames share the same ductile-iron gearbox with two forward gears and one reverse, the same friction-plate clutch, and the same 24 dryland rotary blades in 3+1 groups. If your season includes ridging rows or cutting a drain, look at the attachments before you look at a second machine.

A sensible specification for a one-hectare holding

For roughly one hectare worked by hand, the choice is between the 178cc diesel 1WG-4.1A — 4 kW / 5-15 HP @ 3600 rpm, 800-1050 mm (adjustable) — and the 170cc petrol 1WG-4.0AQ if the work is lighter and the fuel is easier to get. Add the ditcher attachment, and most of the year is covered.

Every machine carries a 1 year warranty, and the range shares its wear parts, so a mixed fleet is simple to maintain. If you tell us your crop, your plot size and who will be operating the machine, we can point you at the right one — the micro tiller range lists every model, and the home page has the enquiry form.

Frequently asked questions

Answers to the questions buyers ask us most.

Which tiller suits a one-hectare holding?

Two models cover most of it: the 178cc diesel 1WG-4.1A at 4 kW / 5-15 HP @ 3600 rpm and 800-1050 mm (adjustable), or the 170cc petrol 1WG-4.0AQ if the soil is light and the work is less continuous.

Can one person carry a micro tiller?

The lighter machines, yes. The 170cc petrol 1WG-4.0AQ weighs 68 kg, which is the difference between a machine one person can lift into a pickup and one that needs two. The 178cc diesel 1WG-4.1A at 115 kg is a two-person lift for most operators.

Should a smallholder buy a second machine or attachments?

Attachments, usually. Every tiller frame is built to take a ridger and a ditcher, and depending on the model a potato digger, a water pump or a trailer. One engine and one gearbox to service covers more of the season than two machines.

How do I choose between petrol and diesel on a smallholding?

Start with what fuel you can actually buy near you, because a machine is only useful if the fuel is available when the ground is ready. Then choose diesel for continuous daily work and petrol for lighter work where weight and fuel availability matter more.

Is a 170cc tiller enough for hard soil?

In wet or heavy ground the answer is usually a diesel machine, because a compression-ignition engine holds its speed when the blades meet resistance. The 170cc petrol 1WG-4.0AQ is a good machine for light soil and for ground that is worked at the right moisture.

How deep can these machines dig?

Working depth is adjustable on every model. The 170cc petrol 1WG-4.0AQ digs to 100-250 mm, and the 186cc diesel 1WG-6.3A to 180-350 mm.

Do all the small tillers share the same wear parts?

Yes. The range shares a ductile-iron gearbox with two forward gears and one reverse, a friction-plate clutch, and 24 dryland rotary blades in 3+1 groups, so a mixed fleet is simple to maintain.

What warranty do the machines carry?

Every machine in the range carries a 1 year warranty.
