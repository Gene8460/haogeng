Self-Priming vs Centrifugal Pump: Which Body Type Do You Need?

Published: 2026-11-15Our Products: Water Pump

The difference is one question: is there water at the inlet?

Every small engine pump in our range shares the same basic layout — an air-cooled four-stroke engine on a frame, a pump body with an inlet and an outlet, a fuel tank and a handle. The two body types we build differ in exactly one respect, and it is the one that decides whether a pump works at all in your situation.

A centrifugal pump needs water at the inlet. A self-priming pump does not.

How a centrifugal pump behaves

The centrifugal pump is the simpler machine and the smaller one. The impeller spins, and the water already sitting in the casing is thrown outward, creating the pressure that pushes water up and out of the outlet. Everything about that depends on the casing being full.

The 2-inch Centrifugal Pump delivers 36 m³/h (600 L/min) at up to 30 m, draws 8 m, and weighs 24 kg. The 3-inch Centrifugal Pump moves 60 m³/h (1000 L/min) on a 80 / 80 mm port.

This is the right pump when the water source is already full: a canal, a farm dam, a concrete sump with the inlet submerged, a tank, or a shallow well that never drops below the pump. Prime it, close the valve, and it runs. The 3-inch unit is also the better choice when you need volume — irrigation across a larger area, or filling a tank in a reasonable time.

How a self-priming pump behaves

A self-priming pump adds a chamber and a valve arrangement that lets it draw air out of the casing and pull water up into it on its own. That is why it can sit above the water line and still deliver.

The self-priming unit in our range, Self-Priming Pump, primes in ≤ 90 seconds at 7 m at 8 m, delivers 50 m³/h (834 L/min), and takes a 50-80 / 50-80 mm hose on both inlet and outlet — so it can be adapted to the pipe size available on site. It weighs 27 kg.

This is the pump for an uncertain or seasonal source: a shallow well that drops in the dry season, a pond, a stream, a pit that is not always flooded. It removes the need to keep the inlet submerged.

Other differences worth knowing

  • Port size and flexibility. The centrifugal pumps in our range come with a fixed 50 mm or 80 mm port. The self-priming unit takes a range of hose sizes, which matters when the buyer does not know what pipe will be on site.
  • Engine. The self-priming unit uses the same petrol four-stroke engine family as the 2-inch centrifugal — 4.5 kW / 6.0 HP @ 3600 rpm and 208 cc — so servicing knowledge transfers between them.
  • Diesel versions exist for both. For continuous duty, the Diesel Well Pump gives 50 m³/h (834 L/min) with a 7 m suction lift, and the double-impeller diesel Double-Impeller handles 70 m³/h (1167 L/min) at up to 50 m with electric starting.
  • Shared wet end. Both types use a cast-iron impeller, an aluminium-alloy pump body and a ceramic-and-graphite mechanical seal. The parts that wear are the same, which matters to anyone maintaining several machines.

Which one to specify

Ask: does the water arrive at the pump without being pumped?

  • Yes — the source is always full. Specify a centrifugal pump. It is lighter, simpler and usually the better value. Choose the 3-inch Centrifugal Pump if you need volume, the 2-inch centrifugal for general work, and the Fire Pump when the priority is head rather than flow — it reaches 60 m.
  • No — or you are not sure. Specify the Self-Priming Pump. The extra capability costs little and removes a common reason a pump arrives and will not work.
  • Deep borehole. The Diesel Well Pump is built for that duty.

Every pump in the range carries a 1 year warranty. Full specifications are on the water pump pages, and the home page has the enquiry form — send us the lift height, the volume and the source, and we will confirm which body type will do the job.

Frequently asked questions

Answers to the questions buyers ask us most.

What is the difference between a self-priming and a centrifugal pump?

One question decides it: is there water at the inlet? A centrifugal pump needs the casing to be already full, and its impeller throws that water outward to create pressure. A self-priming pump draws the air out of the casing and pulls water up into it on its own, so it can sit above the water line.

How long does the self-priming pump take to prime?

The Self-Priming Pump primes in ≤ 90 seconds at 7 m at 8 m, which removes the need to keep the inlet submerged.

Which pump suits a shallow well that drops in the dry season?

The Self-Priming Pump. If you do not know whether the source will stay flooded — a seasonal pond, a river level that drops, a well with a fluctuating water table — self-priming removes the question entirely.

Can I fit whatever hose is available on site?

The self-priming unit takes a 50-80 / 50-80 mm hose on both inlet and outlet, so it can be adapted to the pipe size available. The centrifugal pumps in the range come with a fixed 50 mm or 80 mm port.

The centrifugal. The 2-inch Centrifugal Pump weighs 24 kg and delivers 36 m³/h (600 L/min), and the 3-inch Centrifugal Pump moves 60 m³/h (1000 L/min) for jobs where volume matters more than anything else.

Which pump should I choose when the source is always full?

A centrifugal pump — a canal, a farm dam, a concrete sump with the inlet submerged, a tank, or a shallow well that never drops below the pump. Prime it, close the valve, and it runs.

Are there diesel versions of both body types?

Yes. For continuous duty, the Diesel Well Pump gives 50 m³/h (834 L/min) with a 7 m suction lift, and the double-impeller diesel Double-Impeller handles 70 m³/h (1167 L/min) with electric starting.

Do the two pump types share wear parts?

Yes. Both use a cast-iron impeller, an aluminium-alloy pump body and a ceramic-and-graphite mechanical seal. The parts that wear are the same, which matters to anyone maintaining several machines.
