Micro Tiller for Orchards and Vineyards: Working Between the Rows

Published: 2026-11-15Our Products: Micro Tiller

Between the rows, the rules are different

Tilling a field is about width and depth. Tilling an orchard or a vineyard is about not damaging the thing you are growing. The machine is the same family of machines, but the working method changes completely, and choosing the wrong machine for the job costs you either the crop or a lot of time.

These are the practices our customers use in orchard and vineyard work.

The frame must fit between the rows

The first constraint is geometric. Measure the distance between the planted rows and allow clearance on both sides; the blades need room to pass without touching the trunks or the trellis posts.

For a narrow row system, the 170cc petrol 1WG-4.0AQ is the natural machine: 600-900 mm (adjustable), 100-250 mm, and 68 kg to lift over a low trellis without helpers. The 170cc petrol Lady 1WG-4.0BQ is the same class with a slightly different frame — 600-1000 mm (adjustable) — and works well where rows are a little wider.

For wider inter-row spacing, the 178cc diesel 1WG-4.1A covers 800-1050 mm (adjustable) and digs to 150-300 mm, which suits established orchards with more space between the rows.

The working depth has to be adjustable, not fixed

Orchard soil is not uniform. Under the trees the roots are shallow and the soil is compacted by traffic; between the trees there may be grass cover that needs taking out. A machine with an adjustable depth lets one operator change the setting as they move down the row instead of stopping to reconfigure.

All of our tillers have an adjustable depth setting, from 100-250 mm on the 170cc petrol up to 180-350 mm on the 186cc diesel 1WG-6.3A.

Handle control matters more than horsepower

In a vineyard the operator is walking at the speed of a person, not a tractor. What limits the work is how precisely the machine can be steered and how easily it can be lifted and turned at the end of a row.

This is where the lighter machines earn their place. The 170cc petrol 1WG-4.0AQ weighs 68 kg, which is the difference between a machine one person can lift into a trailer and one that needs two. The 173cc petrol 1WG-4.05B at 78 kg is still light but has more capacity for hard, dry soil under the vines.

The higher-powered machines earn their place in a different orchard job: the heavy work between the blocks — cover cropping, turning a strip of ground, cutting a drainage line along the headland. For that, the 178cc diesel 1WG-4.1A or the 186cc diesel 1WG-6.3A is the right tool.

Never work wet ground, whatever the machine

This advice is the same for every crop, and it is worth repeating for orchards in particular: a tiller working soil that is too wet destroys the structure, and in a permanent crop you cannot easily put that structure back. The blades will smear the ground instead of cutting it, and the compaction you cause stays for years.

Wait for the soil to break cleanly in your hand before starting. The same rule applies to our ditching machines when they cut a drain along an orchard edge.

Attachments for orchard work

Two attachments earn their keep in permanent crops. The ridger is used to build up a bank against the row, and the ditcher cuts the drainage line at the headland or between blocks. Both fit the standard tiller frame, and on the 170cc Dragon base the frame changes between the two in under 10 minutes.

Where water has to be moved as well as soil, our water pumps cover irrigation and dewatering duties in the same season.

Every machine in the range carries a 1 year warranty, and because the range shares a common design, the wear parts are shared across models. Tell us your row spacing, your crop and the type of soil, and we will tell you which machine fits the rows — the micro tiller range lists every model and the home page has the enquiry form.

Frequently asked questions

Answers to the questions buyers ask us most.

What width of tiller do I need between orchard rows?

Measure your actual row spacing and allow clearance on both sides for the blades to pass. The narrowest machine in the range is the 170cc petrol 1WG-4.0AQ at 600-900 mm (adjustable); the 178cc diesel 1WG-4.1A covers 800-1050 mm (adjustable) for wider spacing.

Can the working depth be adjusted on these machines?

Yes, on every model in the range. It runs from 100-250 mm on the 170cc petrol up to 180-350 mm on the 186cc diesel 1WG-6.3A, so one operator can change the setting as they move down the row instead of stopping to reconfigure.

Is it safe to till under fruit trees?

Only shallow, and only where you know where the roots are. Roots near the surface are damaged by a deep cut, and a permanent crop cannot easily be replanted. Set the depth low in the tree row and work the ground between the trees at a deeper setting.

Which machine handles the heavier work between the blocks?

The 178cc diesel 1WG-4.1A or the 186cc diesel 1WG-6.3A. Cover cropping, turning a strip of ground and cutting a drainage line along the headland are open-ground jobs that the lighter machines are not built for.

Can I use a tiller in a vineyard where the rows are narrow?

Yes, provided the frame fits. The 170cc petrol 1WG-4.0AQ at 600-900 mm (adjustable) and 68 kg is the machine that can be lifted over a low trellis without helpers, which is what repeated work down a row actually needs.

Do orchards need drainage?

Often yes, and it is the job that decides the machine. The 170cc Dragon ditching machine 1WG-4.0ZL cuts 250-400 mm deep and 200-400 mm (adjustable) wide, and the ridger and ditcher attachments both fit the standard tiller frame.

Should I ever till wet soil in an orchard?

No, and this matters more in a permanent crop than in an annual one. A tiller working soil that is too wet smears the ground instead of cutting it, and the compaction you cause stays for years. Wait for the soil to break cleanly in your hand before starting.

Which attachments earn their keep in orchard work?

The ridger, to build up a bank against the row, and the ditcher, to cut the drainage line at the headland or between blocks. On the 170cc Dragon base the frame changes between the two in under 10 minutes.
