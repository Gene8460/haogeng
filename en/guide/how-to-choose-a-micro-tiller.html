How to Choose a Micro Tiller for Small Farms

Published: 2026-11-15Our Products: Micro Tiller

Start with three questions about the land

Most buyers start by comparing horsepower, and end up over- or under-buying. The three questions below narrow the range faster than any specification table.

How much ground do you need to cover in one pass?

Working width is the number that decides how long a job takes. On our machines the working width is given as an adjustable range rather than a single fixed figure.

  • 600-900 mm — comfortable between vegetable rows, in a garden plot, or between orchard rows.
  • 800-1050 mm — the common size for smallholder plots worked from one end to the other.
  • 900-1200 mm — for open ground where the priority is covering area per hour.

For comparison, the entry model 1WG-4.0AQ tills 600-900 mm (adjustable) at a depth of 100-250 mm, while the widest diesel machine in the range, 1WG-6.3A, works 900-1200 mm (adjustable). Neither is "better" — they are sized for different plots.

What are you actually growing?

The same machine behaves differently depending on the crop and the soil:

  • Seedbed preparation for vegetables — a shallow, adjustable depth matters more than power.
  • Inter-row cultivation in orchards and vineyards — a narrower frame and a controllable handle matter more than width.
  • Row crops and heavy soil — the diesel models are built for this, because compression-ignition engines hold speed under load and the ductile-iron gearbox takes sustained work.

Where will the fuel come from?

This is the question that decides petrol versus diesel, and it is not a technical one. Choose the fuel you can actually buy in your market, and then the machine size that follows from your plot.

Petrol or diesel: the trade-off in plain terms

Our petrol machines are lighter and cheaper to buy. Our diesel machines weigh more, but the engine is compression-ignition, which means lower fuel consumption and steadier output under load. On the 170cc diesel 1WG-4.0A the fuel tank holds 3.5 L and the machine itself weighs 95 kg. On the 170cc petrol 1WG-4.0AQ the tank is 3.6 L and the machine weighs only 68 kg — a difference you feel every time you load it into a pickup.

Both are air-cooled, single-cylinder, four-stroke or two-stroke engines. Most models start by manual recoil, so they need no battery and can be started in the field without tools; the 186 Tiger 1WG-6.3B-T adds electric starting on top of the recoil starter.

Engine size: what actually changes between 170 and 186 cc

Displacement is the honest way to compare these machines, because it tells you how much work the engine can do before it has to labour:

  • 170cc — 2.9 kW / 5-15 HP @ 3600 rpm on the diesel version, 4 kW / 5-15 HP @ 3600 rpm on the petrol version. Enough for gardens, vegetable plots and orchard rows.
  • 173cc — 3.5 kW / 5-15 HP @ 3600 rpm on the diesel 1WG-4.05A, 4.05 kW / 5-15 HP @ 3600 rpm on the petrol 1WG-4.05B. A common middle size in our range.
  • 178cc — 4 kW / 5-15 HP @ 3600 rpm on the diesel 1WG-4.1A.
  • 186cc — 6.3 kW / 5-15 HP @ 3600 rpm on the diesel 1WG-6.3A, working 900-1200 mm (adjustable). This is the machine for open fields and daily commercial work.

All of them share the same heavy-duty construction: a ductile-iron gearbox with two forward gears and one reverse, a friction-plate clutch, and 24 dryland rotary blades in 3+1 groups. The gearbox is the part that decides how long the machine survives daily work, and it is the same design across the range.

One machine, several jobs

Every tiller in the range is compatible with a ridger and a ditcher. Depending on the model, the same frame also takes a potato digger, a water pump or a trailer. If your season includes ridging rows or cutting drainage channels, that is usually a cheaper way to cover the work than buying a second machine.

If your work is mostly canal and drainage maintenance rather than soil preparation, have a look at our ditching machines — they are built around that job rather than around the tiller frame.

What to check before you order

  • Frame width against your rows. Measure the distance between planted rows and add clearance.
  • Fuel availability. Look up what your local market actually stocks before choosing the engine.
  • Transport. Check the machine weight against what you can load and unload. Our smallest petrol tiller is 68 kg; the heaviest diesel tiller in the range is 140 kg.
  • Working depth. All models are adjustable, but the useful range differs — check the depth specification against your soil.

Warranty and parts

Every machine in the range carries a 1 year warranty. The range is built to a common design, so consumables and wear parts are shared across models.

Frequently asked questions

Answers to the questions buyers ask us most.

What is the difference between a micro tiller and a walking tractor?

A micro tiller is a single-operator machine with a compact frame and rotary blades; it is carried or guided by the operator while walking behind it. A walking tractor is a larger, ride-on or walk-behind unit with a full transmission and a working width measured in metres rather than millimetres. For plots up to a couple of hectares a micro tiller is the usual choice.

Can one micro tiller both till the soil and cut drainage channels?

Yes. The tiller frame accepts a ridger and a ditcher, and on some models a potato digger, a water pump or a trailer as well, so one machine can cover seedbed preparation, row ridging and channel cutting.

Do I need a battery to start the machine?

Almost never. Most models in our range use manual recoil starting only, so there is no battery or electric starter to maintain in the field. The 186 Tiger model adds electric starting alongside the recoil starter.

How deep can these machines dig?

Working depth is adjustable on every model. For example the 173cc diesel 1WG-4.05A works at 150-300 mm, and the 170cc petrol 1WG-4.0AQ at 100-250 mm.

Which model should I choose for a plot of about one hectare?

For roughly one hectare, a 173cc or 178cc machine with an 800-1050 mm working width covers ground quickly while staying easy to handle. The 170cc models are the lighter option for garden-scale work; the 186cc models suit open ground and daily commercial use.

What warranty do the machines carry?

Every machine carries a 1 year warranty, the same across the range.
