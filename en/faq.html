Frequently asked questions

Answers to the questions buyers ask us most.

What is the difference between a micro tiller and a walking tractor?

A micro tiller is a single-operator machine with a compact frame and rotary blades; it is carried or guided by the operator while walking behind it. A walking tractor is a larger, ride-on or walk-behind unit with a full transmission and a working width measured in metres rather than millimetres. For plots up to a couple of hectares a micro tiller is the usual choice.

Can one micro tiller both till the soil and cut drainage channels?

Yes. The tiller frame accepts a ridger and a ditcher, and on some models a potato digger, a water pump or a trailer as well, so one machine can cover seedbed preparation, row ridging and channel cutting.

Do I need a battery to start the machine?

Almost never. Most models in our range use manual recoil starting only, so there is no battery or electric starter to maintain in the field. The 186 Tiger model adds electric starting alongside the recoil starter.

How deep can these machines dig?

Working depth is adjustable on every model. For example the 173cc diesel 1WG-4.05A works at 150-300 mm, and the 170cc petrol 1WG-4.0AQ at 100-250 mm.

Which model should I choose for a plot of about one hectare?

For roughly one hectare, a 173cc or 178cc machine with an 800-1050 mm working width covers ground quickly while staying easy to handle. The 170cc models are the lighter option for garden-scale work; the 186cc models suit open ground and daily commercial use.

What warranty do the machines carry?

Every machine carries a 1 year warranty, the same across the range.

Is a diesel micro tiller always more economical?

A diesel engine generally burns less fuel for the same amount of work and diesel is often cheaper per litre in agricultural markets, so running cost usually favours diesel. The petrol machines still win on purchase price and on weight.

Can I convert a petrol micro tiller to diesel?

No. The engine mounting, fuel tank, fuel lines and drive are all dimensioned for one fuel type, and a conversion is not something we support.

Which is lighter, the petrol or the diesel tiller?

The petrol models. The 170cc petrol 1WG-4.0AQ weighs 68 kg, while the 170cc diesel 1WG-4.0A weighs 95 kg. The gap narrows on the larger frames but never reverses.

Does a diesel tiller pull harder in wet soil?

A compression-ignition diesel engine holds its speed when the blades meet resistance, because it does not depend on a spark as the rpm falls. In wet or heavy ground that is the difference between finishing a row and stopping to clear the tines.

Do your diesel tillers have electric starting?

Most start by manual recoil. The 186cc Tiger 1WG-6.3B-T adds electric starting on top of the recoil starter.

Which fuel should a smallholder choose?

Choose the fuel that is available and affordable, then the size that fits the plot. The 170cc petrol 1WG-4.0AQ at 68 kg is the easiest to move by hand; the 178cc diesel 1WG-4.1A is the better daily machine.

Do petrol and diesel tillers share the same gearbox?

Yes. Every tiller in the range uses a ductile-iron gearbox with two forward gears and one reverse and a friction-plate clutch, so wear parts are shared across the fuel types.

Do you build ditching machines and pumps in both fuel types too?

Yes. Our ditching machines and our water pumps cover petrol and diesel variants, because the fuel available differs from one market to the next.

Which engine size should a distributor put at the front of the range?

The 178cc diesel 1WG-4.1A. It is the size that carries the most daily tillage work, and it is still light enough for two people to load. The 170cc petrol 1WG-4.0AQ covers the small end of the market, and the 186cc diesel 1WG-6.3A covers open-field work.

Is a 173cc tiller better than a 170cc?

Not in a way that matters to most buyers. The 173cc is a middle specification that fills the gap between the 170 and the 178, and it is a machine to consider when you know the market wants that size. It is not the size we would put at the front of a new range.

Does a bigger engine mean a wider blade?

No. Working width is a frame decision, not an engine decision. The 170cc petrol 1WG-4.0AQ works 600-900 mm (adjustable) on a smaller frame than the 178cc diesel 1WG-4.1A at 800-1050 mm (adjustable), and that difference comes from the machine, not the displacement alone.

Do all four sizes use the same gearbox?

Yes. Every tiller in the range uses a ductile-iron gearbox with two forward gears and one reverse, a friction-plate clutch, and 24 dryland rotary blades in 3+1 groups. The gearbox is the part that decides how long the machine lasts.

What is the difference between the 186cc 1WG-6.3A and the 186cc Tiger 1WG-6.3B-T?

The Tiger adds electric starting on top of the recoil starter and weighs 140 kg. It is the same 186cc class of engine, chosen where operators start and stop the machine many times a day.

What is the maximum digging depth across the range?

Depth is adjustable on every model. It runs from 100-250 mm on the 170cc petrol up to 180-350 mm on the 186cc diesel 1WG-6.3A.

Can I order petrol and diesel models in the same shipment?

Yes. The petrol machines are led by the 170cc 1WG-4.0AQ and the 173cc 1WG-4.05B, and the diesel machines run from the 170cc 1WG-4.0A to the 186cc 1WG-6.3B-T. They share wear parts and the same gearbox design, so a mixed container is straightforward to service.

How much more work does the 186cc do than the 178cc?

The 186cc diesel 1WG-6.3A is rated at 6.3 kW / 5-15 HP @ 3600 rpm against 4 kW / 5-15 HP @ 3600 rpm for the 178cc diesel 1WG-4.1A, and it covers more ground per pass at 900-1200 mm (adjustable) against 800-1050 mm (adjustable). It is also the heavier machine, which decides who can load it.

Is a ditching machine just a bigger version of a tiller?

No. A tiller works the soil with rotary blades, 24 dryland blades in 3+1 groups on every machine in our range. A ditching machine cuts a channel with a ditching wheel or disc that throws the soil to one side as it moves forward. One prepares a seedbed, the other cuts a trench.

Can one machine do both tilling and ditching?

Yes. The tiller frames are built to take a ridger and a ditcher, and on the 170cc Dragon base the frame changes between the two in under 10 minutes. For a customer who needs both jobs in one season, one machine with two attachments is usually the more sensible order than two machines.

Which machine is right for rice paddies and sugarcane?

Drainage and irrigation channels dominate that work, so the ditching machine range. The 178cc Bull 1WG-4.1ZN cuts 300-500 mm deep and 200-400 mm adjustable wide, and the 186cc Bull Pro 1WG-6.3ZN goes deeper still at 350-600 mm.

Do the ditching machines use the same gearbox as the tillers?

Yes. They run on the same air-cooled single-cylinder engine family, the same ductile-iron gearbox with two forward gears and one reverse, and the same friction-plate clutch. The 178cc Bull 1WG-4.1ZN is rated at 4.0 kW / 5.4 HP @ 3600 rpm and weighs 118 kg.

Which model should a distributor stock first?

Look at the crop rather than the market. Soil preparation dominates for vegetable growing, cereals, cotton and maize, so the tiller range, anchored on the 178cc diesel 1WG-4.1A. For rice, sugarcane and plantation crops, stock the ditching range, anchored on the 178cc Bull 1WG-4.1ZN.

Do I need a pump as well as a ditching machine?

Often, yes. A drainage scheme usually needs moving water as well as cut channels, and our water pump range includes petrol and diesel units for irrigation and dewatering. Many customers running ditching machines run them alongside a pump for exactly that reason.

How deep can the ditching machine cut?

The 170cc Dragon 1WG-4.0ZL cuts 250-400 mm deep and 200-400 mm (adjustable) wide, the 178cc Bull 1WG-4.1ZN reaches 300-500 mm and 200-400 mm adjustable, and the 186cc Bull Pro 1WG-6.3ZN reaches 350-600 mm.

What warranty do these machines carry?

Every machine carries a 1 year warranty.

Which tiller suits a one-hectare holding?

Two models cover most of it: the 178cc diesel 1WG-4.1A at 4 kW / 5-15 HP @ 3600 rpm and 800-1050 mm (adjustable), or the 170cc petrol 1WG-4.0AQ if the soil is light and the work is less continuous.

Can one person carry a micro tiller?

The lighter machines, yes. The 170cc petrol 1WG-4.0AQ weighs 68 kg, which is the difference between a machine one person can lift into a pickup and one that needs two. The 178cc diesel 1WG-4.1A at 115 kg is a two-person lift for most operators.

Should a smallholder buy a second machine or attachments?

Attachments, usually. Every tiller frame is built to take a ridger and a ditcher, and depending on the model a potato digger, a water pump or a trailer. One engine and one gearbox to service covers more of the season than two machines.

How do I choose between petrol and diesel on a smallholding?

Start with what fuel you can actually buy near you, because a machine is only useful if the fuel is available when the ground is ready. Then choose diesel for continuous daily work and petrol for lighter work where weight and fuel availability matter more.

Is a 170cc tiller enough for hard soil?

In wet or heavy ground the answer is usually a diesel machine, because a compression-ignition engine holds its speed when the blades meet resistance. The 170cc petrol 1WG-4.0AQ is a good machine for light soil and for ground that is worked at the right moisture.

How deep can these machines dig?

Working depth is adjustable on every model. The 170cc petrol 1WG-4.0AQ digs to 100-250 mm, and the 186cc diesel 1WG-6.3A to 180-350 mm.

Do all the small tillers share the same wear parts?

Yes. The range shares a ductile-iron gearbox with two forward gears and one reverse, a friction-plate clutch, and 24 dryland rotary blades in 3+1 groups, so a mixed fleet is simple to maintain.

What warranty do the machines carry?

Every machine in the range carries a 1 year warranty.

What width of tiller do I need between orchard rows?

Measure your actual row spacing and allow clearance on both sides for the blades to pass. The narrowest machine in the range is the 170cc petrol 1WG-4.0AQ at 600-900 mm (adjustable); the 178cc diesel 1WG-4.1A covers 800-1050 mm (adjustable) for wider spacing.

Can the working depth be adjusted on these machines?

Yes, on every model in the range. It runs from 100-250 mm on the 170cc petrol up to 180-350 mm on the 186cc diesel 1WG-6.3A, so one operator can change the setting as they move down the row instead of stopping to reconfigure.

Is it safe to till under fruit trees?

Only shallow, and only where you know where the roots are. Roots near the surface are damaged by a deep cut, and a permanent crop cannot easily be replanted. Set the depth low in the tree row and work the ground between the trees at a deeper setting.

Which machine handles the heavier work between the blocks?

The 178cc diesel 1WG-4.1A or the 186cc diesel 1WG-6.3A. Cover cropping, turning a strip of ground and cutting a drainage line along the headland are open-ground jobs that the lighter machines are not built for.

Can I use a tiller in a vineyard where the rows are narrow?

Yes, provided the frame fits. The 170cc petrol 1WG-4.0AQ at 600-900 mm (adjustable) and 68 kg is the machine that can be lifted over a low trellis without helpers, which is what repeated work down a row actually needs.

Do orchards need drainage?

Often yes, and it is the job that decides the machine. The 170cc Dragon ditching machine 1WG-4.0ZL cuts 250-400 mm deep and 200-400 mm (adjustable) wide, and the ridger and ditcher attachments both fit the standard tiller frame.

Should I ever till wet soil in an orchard?

No, and this matters more in a permanent crop than in an annual one. A tiller working soil that is too wet smears the ground instead of cutting it, and the compaction you cause stays for years. Wait for the soil to break cleanly in your hand before starting.

Which attachments earn their keep in orchard work?

The ridger, to build up a bank against the row, and the ditcher, to cut the drainage line at the headland or between blocks. On the 170cc Dragon base the frame changes between the two in under 10 minutes.

What information do I need to choose a water pump?

Four numbers: how much water you need per hour, how high it has to be lifted, how far it has to travel, and what the source is. Add the vertical lift, the height of every bend and elbow, and a little extra for pipe friction, then compare that figure with the maximum total head of the pump.

How much water does the 2-inch pump deliver?

The 2-inch Centrifugal Pump delivers 36 m³/h (600 L/min) on a 50 / 50 mm port, up to 30 m total head.

Which pump is right for a deep borehole?

The Diesel Well Pump is rated at 8.8 kW / 11.8 HP @ 3600 rpm with a 7 m suction lift and a 5.5 L tank, at 100 kg.

Which pump delivers the most water?

The double-impeller diesel pump, at 70 m³/h (1167 L/min) and up to 50 m, on diesel for continuous duty.

Should I buy a petrol or a diesel pump?

Petrol is the answer for intermittent use and light duty. Diesel is the answer for a pump that runs all day, every day, and where petrol storage is a problem. The petrol 2-inch Centrifugal Pump weighs 24 kg, while the diesel double-impeller pump weighs 78 kg.

Does the diesel pump have electric starting?

The double-impeller diesel pump starts with electric + recoil, which suits an operator running it through a long day.

Which pump works when the source is not always flooded?

The Self-Priming Pump primes itself in ≤ 90 seconds at 7 m at 8 m, so the inlet does not have to stay submerged.

Do all your pumps share the same wet end?

Yes. A cast-iron impeller, an aluminium-alloy pump body and a ceramic-and-graphite mechanical seal across the range. That is the part that has to survive grit and running dry, and every pump carries a 1 year warranty.

What is the difference between a self-priming and a centrifugal pump?

One question decides it: is there water at the inlet? A centrifugal pump needs the casing to be already full, and its impeller throws that water outward to create pressure. A self-priming pump draws the air out of the casing and pulls water up into it on its own, so it can sit above the water line.

How long does the self-priming pump take to prime?

The Self-Priming Pump primes in ≤ 90 seconds at 7 m at 8 m, which removes the need to keep the inlet submerged.

Which pump suits a shallow well that drops in the dry season?

The Self-Priming Pump. If you do not know whether the source will stay flooded — a seasonal pond, a river level that drops, a well with a fluctuating water table — self-priming removes the question entirely.

Can I fit whatever hose is available on site?

The self-priming unit takes a 50-80 / 50-80 mm hose on both inlet and outlet, so it can be adapted to the pipe size available. The centrifugal pumps in the range come with a fixed 50 mm or 80 mm port.

Which pump is simpler and lighter?

The centrifugal. The 2-inch Centrifugal Pump weighs 24 kg and delivers 36 m³/h (600 L/min), and the 3-inch Centrifugal Pump moves 60 m³/h (1000 L/min) for jobs where volume matters more than anything else.

Which pump should I choose when the source is always full?

A centrifugal pump — a canal, a farm dam, a concrete sump with the inlet submerged, a tank, or a shallow well that never drops below the pump. Prime it, close the valve, and it runs.

Are there diesel versions of both body types?

Yes. For continuous duty, the Diesel Well Pump gives 50 m³/h (834 L/min) with a 7 m suction lift, and the double-impeller diesel Double-Impeller handles 70 m³/h (1167 L/min) with electric starting.

Do the two pump types share wear parts?

Yes. Both use a cast-iron impeller, an aluminium-alloy pump body and a ceramic-and-graphite mechanical seal. The parts that wear are the same, which matters to anyone maintaining several machines.

How often should a micro tiller be serviced?

Three intervals. Five minutes before every working day to check fuel, oil, the air filter, the fuel tap and lines, the blades and the guards. About an hour weekly on the drive, the greased joints, the blades, the gearbox oil and the fuel filter. Monthly and at the end of the season for the air cleaner, the spark plug, the carburettor, the gearbox oil and off-season storage.

What is the most common cause of a tiller that will not start?

The fuel system. Check the tank, that the tap is open, that no water or dirt has collected in the filter bowl, then the air filter, the choke and the spark plug. Water in the fuel is what turns a machine that started yesterday into one that will not start today.

Why does the engine run rough and burn fuel badly?

A blocked air filter. It is the most common reason for it and also the cheapest thing to fix. Remove and clean the filter element rather than just blowing on it.

How often should I check the gearbox oil?

Weekly and again monthly. Look for metal in it as well as the level. Fresh oil with metal in it means something inside has been wearing, and it is worth finding out before it becomes a failure.

Which spare parts should I keep in the toolbox?

Spare blades and tines, a drive belt or chain set, a set of standard bolts and nuts, a spark plug, an air filter element, a fuel filter, and a good quality fuel line. Keep them in the vehicle, not in a warehouse, because a machine that stops in a field is only fixed by what is in the vehicle with it.

How do I store the machine over the dry season?

Drain the fuel if the machine will stand for weeks, clean the machine, oil the exposed metal, cover the intake, and store it dry and off the ground. A machine stored damp will show rust from the first month.

What causes vibration in a micro tiller?

Almost always a bent tine or a worn blade group. Check the blades before you look anywhere else, and balance the set if a group is noticeably worn on one side.

Do all your machines share the same maintenance routine?

Yes. The 170cc petrol 1WG-4.0AQ, the 178cc diesel 1WG-4.1A and the 186cc diesel 1WG-6.3A all share the same construction, and because the tillers, the ditching machines and the water pumps share a common design, wear parts and consumables are consistent across models. Every machine carries a 1 year warranty.

What order do I start a micro tiller in?

Level ground, in gear and clear of people; fuel and oil checked and the fuel tap open; controls in neutral with the throttle closed and the depth lever at its shallowest; clutch engaged; choke closed if the engine is cold; then pull the recoil starter firmly and smoothly. Open the choke as soon as the engine catches and let it run for a minute.

Should the choke be closed when starting a cold engine?

Yes. A cold start needs a richer mixture, and that is what the choke is for. Open it as soon as the engine catches.

Why does the engine stall when the blades meet resistance?

Check the fuel and air side first, in this order: fuel in the tank, fuel tap open, air filter clean, choke used correctly, spark plug clean. On a diesel, also check the fuel filter for water.

How do I set the working depth?

With the depth lever on the handle, and only with the engine stopped. Start shallow on the first pass and deepen it as the soil proves easy to work. A machine set too deep on the first pass fights the operator and can bury itself.

Which gear should I use for new ground?

The low gear for breaking new ground and heavy soil, the high gear for the finish pass and for moving across the plot. The reverse gets you out of a corner or a rutted patch without turning the machine around.

What should I do if the machine hits a stone or a root?

Release the clutch and lift the handle slightly rather than forcing it. Forcing the machine digs in and stalls the engine; lifting the tines and easing forward is faster.

Can I work the soil when it is wet?

No. Working soil that is too wet smears it instead of cutting it, and it packs down again as it dries. Wait for the soil to break cleanly in your hand before starting.

Is it safe to stop the engine with the tines in the soil?

No. Take the throttle down, disengage the clutch and let the engine idle before switching it off. The rebound from stopping with the tines buried can damage the drive.
